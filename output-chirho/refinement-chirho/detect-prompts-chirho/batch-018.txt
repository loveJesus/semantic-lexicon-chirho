# Sense Refinement Detection — Batch 018

You are a biblical lexicographer reviewing sense entries for a Hebrew/Aramaic/Greek lexicon.
Each entry below is a single "sense" that was produced by clustering translation signatures 
and then merging with an LLM. Some senses may have been OVER-LUMPED — they contain genuinely 
distinct meanings that should be separate senses.

## Your Task

For each sense, determine if it should be SPLIT into multiple senses. Look for:
- Literal vs. figurative/metaphorical uses lumped together
- Concrete vs. abstract meanings combined
- Multiple distinct referents (e.g., "father" = biological parent + patron/advisor + originator)
- Physical vs. spiritual/theological uses merged
- Different syntactic frames producing different meanings

## Output Format

Return a JSON array. For senses that need NO splitting, omit them entirely.
For senses that SHOULD be split, include an entry like:

```json
[
  {
    "senseIdChirho": 79287,
    "lemmaIdChirho": "H0001",
    "currentLabelChirho": "father, immediate male parent",
    "splitProposalChirho": [
      {
        "newLabelChirho": "biological/legal father",
        "criteriaChirho": "Direct parent-child relationship, possessive constructions with specific person"
      },
      {
        "newLabelChirho": "metaphorical father (patron, advisor, protector)",
        "criteriaChirho": "Title of respect for prophets, advisors, or authority figures; 'father to Pharaoh' type usage"
      }
    ],
    "confidenceChirho": "high",
    "reasoningChirho": "Description explicitly mentions 'title of respect or metaphorical originator' alongside biological father"
  }
]
```

Only propose splits where you have MEDIUM or HIGH confidence. Do NOT split function words (articles, 
prepositions, particles, conjunctions) unless there is a genuinely distinct semantic sense (not just 
syntactic variation). Proper nouns generally should NOT be split.

If NO senses in this batch need splitting, return an empty array: []

**Lemma gloss:** To drink — consume liquid; figuratively, to partake of suffering, judgment, or spiritual sustenance.
**Description:** To consume a liquid by drinking, used both literally of drinking water, wine, or other beverages, and figuratively of partaking in suffering (drinking the cup), spiritual sustenance (drinking living water, drinking Christ's blood), or divine judgment.
**Enriched:** To drink or consume a liquid, used literally of drinking water, wine, or other beverages in everyday and festive contexts (Matt 6:25; 11:18-19; Luke 7:33-34; John 2:10), and figuratively of partaking in Christ's suffering (Matt 20:22; Mark 10:38 — 'drinking the cup'), receiving spiritual sustenance (1 Cor 10:4; John 7:37), and sharing in the eucharistic covenant (Matt 26:27-29; 1 Cor 11:25). All target languages use their standard verb for drinking, confirming a unified semantic core.

### H0730 / Sense 84091 (73 occ, sense 1 of 1)
**Label:** cedar tree or wood
**Lemma gloss:** Cedar — the cedar tree (Cedrus libani), famed for its height, fragrance, and prized timber; a symbol of majesty and pride.
**Description:** The cedar tree (Cedrus libani), especially the cedar of Lebanon, used both for the living tree and its timber as building material.
**Enriched:** The cedar tree (Cedrus libani), encompassing the living tree growing on Lebanon (Ps 104:16; Judg 9:15; Isa 2:13), its harvested timber used for temples, palaces, and ships (1 Kgs 5:6-10; 6:15; Ezek 27:5), and its ritual use in purification ceremonies alongside hyssop (Lev 14:4, 49-52). Figuratively, the cedar represents towering majesty (Ezek 31:3-14; Num 24:6) and, when felled by God, the humbling of human pride (Isa 2:13; Zech 11:1-2). Cognates across Aramaic, Arabic, and Ethiopic confirm a shared Semitic term.

### H1288 / Sense 88188 (73 occ, sense 2 of 7)

**Lemma gloss:** To bless, kneel; Piel: invoke blessing; Qal passive: blessed; euphemistically: curse, blaspheme
**Description:** Adjectival state of being blessed, used predicatively or as an exclamation of praise (Qal passive participle barukh); Arabic mubarak, Korean bok-issda, conveying a state rather than an action.

### H3225 / Sense 102422 (73 occ, sense 1 of 3)
**Label:** right side (direction / position)
**Lemma gloss:** The right hand, right side, or south; denoting spatial direction, the hand as instrument of power, and the southern cardinal direction.
**Description:** The right as a spatial direction or positional location, including 'at someone's right' as a place of honor or proximity; cross-linguistically distinguished by Spanish 'derecha' (directional, vs 'diestra'), Swahili 'kulia' or 'upande wa kulia' without 'mkono' (no hand reference), Korean 오른쪽 (oreunjjok, 'right side') or 오른편 (oreunpyeon, 'right area'), and Arabic يَمِين (yamīn) with directional prepositions عَن/مِن (from/at).

Spanish derecha and French droite consistently select directional vocabulary here, while German nach-rechts and Korean 오른쪽 confirm the spatial-orientational framing distinct from the body-part sense.

### H5012 / Sense 115455 (73 occ, sense 1 of 2)
**Label:** prophesy, deliver oracle
**Lemma gloss:** To prophesy -- speaking God's word by divine authority (Nifal) or prophesying ecstatically under Spirit compulsion (Hitpael).
**Description:** To speak or deliver a message as a prophet under divine authority, conveying God's word in the Nifal stem (standard prophetic speech, commands to prophesy, participial 'the one who prophesies').
**Enriched:** Prophesying in the Nifal stem (73x) -- the standard form for delivering divine oracles, commands to prophesy, and participial descriptions of 'the one who prophesies.' This is the voice of classical prophecy: Jeremiah is commanded 'prophesy!' (Jer 19:14; 20:6), Ezekiel speaks over the dry bones (Ezek 37:7), and the Chronicler records levitical prophesying with instruments (1 Chr 25:2-3). Multilingual renderings are straightforward -- Spanish 'profetiza,' French 'prophetise!,' German 'weissagen' -- treating it as authorized divine communication.

### H5676 / Sense 122249 (73 occ, sense 1 of 3)
**Label:** beyond, region across
**Lemma gloss:** The region across or beyond (a river or boundary); a side, flank, or direction relative to an object; plural: sides, edges.
**Description:** The region or area on the other side of a river, boundary, or geographic feature; used with various prepositions (be-/min-/le-) to express location or motion relative to the far side, as in 'beyond the Jordan' or 'from beyond the river'.
**Enriched:** The region across or beyond a geographic feature, especially a river, sea, or border (73 occurrences). Spanish al otro lado de, French au-delà de, and German jenseits all employ compound prepositional phrases, reflecting the inherent two-term spatial relationship: this side versus that side.

**Description:** Niphal passive of עשׂה: to be done, be made, be performed (Arabic yuṣnaʿu يُصنَعُ / yufʿalu يُفعَلُ), expressing that an action or product results without specifying the agent.

### H7592 / Sense 137326 (73 occ, sense 2 of 6)
**Label:** request, petition, demand
**Lemma gloss:** To ask, inquire, request, demand, borrow, or lend; covering question-asking, petition, and the loan transaction in both directions
**Description:** Qal sense of requesting or demanding something from someone, corresponding to Arabic Talaba (طَلَبَ) and Korean guhaetda (구했다), used when seeking to obtain goods, favors, or outcomes rather than information.
**Enriched:** To request, petition, or demand something from someone, seeking to obtain goods, favors, or outcomes rather than information. ### H0499 / Sense 82671 (72 occ, sense 1 of 1)
**Label:** Eleazar (proper name)
**Lemma gloss:** Eleazar — 'God has helped'; a proper name borne by Aaron's son the high priest and several later biblical figures.
**Description:** A masculine proper name meaning 'God has helped' (from אֵל 'God' + עָזַר 'to help'), borne by several individuals in the Hebrew Bible, most prominently: (a) Eleazar son of Aaron, who became high priest after Aaron's death (Exod 6:23, 25; Num 3:2, 4, 32; 16:37, 39; 19:3-4; 20:25-28; 31:29, 41; Josh 14:1; 24:33; Judg 20:28), and (b) other minor figures including Eleazar son of Abinadab (1 Sam 7:1), Eleazar son of Dodo (2 Sam 23:9), and Levites in Chronicles and Ezra. All four clusters represent the same proper noun; the splits arose from prepositional variants (ל 'to/for' Eleazar) and minor transliteration differences (Eleazar vs. Elazar), not from distinct referents requiring separate lexicographic senses.
**Enriched:** The masculine proper name Eleazar ('God has helped'), referring primarily to Aaron's third son who became Israel's second high priest after Aaron's death on Mount Hor (Num 20:25-28; Exod 6:23; Josh 14:1), and secondarily to several later figures: the guardian of the ark (1 Sam 7:1), one of David's mighty warriors (2 Sam 23:9; 1 Chr 11:12), Levites in the temple service (1 Chr 23:21-22), and postexilic priests and laymen (Ezra 8:33; 10:25). All target languages transliterate the name directly.

### H1008 / Sense 86545 (72 occ, sense 1 of 1)
**Label:** Bethel (city)
**Lemma gloss:** Bethel — 'house of God'; an ancient Canaanite city in Ephraim, a major Israelite cultic site tied to Jacob's vision.
**Description:** The ancient Canaanite city renamed by Jacob (formerly Luz), located north of Jerusalem in the hill country of Ephraim; a major cultic site where Jacob saw the ladder to heaven (Gen 28), where the ark of the covenant was kept (Judg 20), and later where Jeroboam set up a golden calf shrine (1 Kgs 12–13).
**Enriched:** The place name Bethel ('house of God'), identifying the ancient city in the hill country of Ephraim where Jacob saw the heavenly ladder and consecrated a pillar (Gen 28:10-19; 35:1-15), where the ark was housed during the judges (Judg 20:26-28), and where Jeroboam I later erected a golden calf, turning it into a center of condemned worship (1 Kgs 12:28-33; Amos 3:14; Hos 10:15). All target languages transliterate the name, though its etymology — beth ('house') + el ('God') — is transparent in Hebrew.
