# Sense Refinement Detection — Batch 023

You are a biblical lexicographer reviewing sense entries for a Hebrew/Aramaic/Greek lexicon.
Each entry below is a single "sense" that was produced by clustering translation signatures 
and then merging with an LLM. Some senses may have been OVER-LUMPED — they contain genuinely 
distinct meanings that should be separate senses.

## Your Task

For each sense, determine if it should be SPLIT into multiple senses. Look for:
- Literal vs. figurative/metaphorical uses lumped together
- Concrete vs. abstract meanings combined
- Multiple distinct referents (e.g., "father" = biological parent + patron/advisor + originator)
- Physical vs. spiritual/theological uses merged
- Different syntactic frames producing different meanings

## Output Format

Return a JSON array. For senses that need NO splitting, omit them entirely.
For senses that SHOULD be split, include an entry like:

```json
[
  {
    "senseIdChirho": 79287,
    "lemmaIdChirho": "H0001",
    "currentLabelChirho": "father, immediate male parent",
    "splitProposalChirho": [
      {
        "newLabelChirho": "biological/legal father",
        "criteriaChirho": "Direct parent-child relationship, possessive constructions with specific person"
      },
      {
        "newLabelChirho": "metaphorical father (patron, advisor, protector)",
        "criteriaChirho": "Title of respect for prophets, advisors, or authority figures; 'father to Pharaoh' type usage"
      }
    ],
    "confidenceChirho": "high",
    "reasoningChirho": "Description explicitly mentions 'title of respect or metaphorical originator' alongside biological father"
  }
]
```

Only propose splits where you have MEDIUM or HIGH confidence. Do NOT split function words (articles, 
prepositions, particles, conjunctions) unless there is a genuinely distinct semantic sense (not just 
syntactic variation). Proper nouns generally should NOT be split.

If NO senses in this batch need splitting, return an empty array: []

### H1836 / Sense 91944 (58 occ, sense 1 of 1)
**Label:** this, that, thus (Aramaic demonstrative)
**Lemma gloss:** This, that, thus — Aramaic demonstrative pronoun used attributively and predicatively in Daniel and Ezra
**Description:** Aramaic demonstrative pronoun meaning 'this,' 'that,' or (with preposition ke-) 'like this, thus.' Extremely common in the Aramaic portions of Ezra and Daniel, functioning as both an attributive and predicative demonstrative. In Ezra 4-5, it appears dozens of times modifying nouns like 'this house,' 'this city,' 'this decree.' In Dan 2:10, no king has asked a thing like this (kidnah). In Dan 2:43, referring to 'that' kingdom. In Dan 5:25 with conjunction 'and this' (udnah). In Jer 10:11 (the sole Aramaic verse in Jeremiah), used adverbially as 'thus' (kidnah). All forms -- bare, with ke-, with waw -- are inflectional variants of a single demonstrative lexeme. Glosses: arb hadhihi/hadha/haakadha, deu dieses, kor igot/iwa gati, spa esta/esto/asi, hin yah/aise, fra ceci.
**Enriched:** Aramaic demonstrative pronoun meaning 'this, that, thus.' All 58 occurrences cluster in Ezra 4–7 and Daniel 2–7, the Aramaic sections of the Hebrew Bible. Spanish distinguishes gender ('este/esta'), French uses 'ceci,' and German 'dieses,' each mapping naturally to a single deictic function. With the preposition כְּ it extends to the adverbial sense 'like this, thus' (Dan 2:10; 7:5). The pronoun anchors discourse references in official correspondence and visionary interpretation alike.

### H1892 / Sense 92409 (58 occ, sense 1 of 3)

**Description:** The abstract, philosophical sense dominant in Ecclesiastes: the fundamental futility, meaninglessness, or absurdity of human endeavor and earthly existence under the sun.
**Enriched:** The dominant abstract sense — vanity, futility, absurdity — the keynote of Ecclesiastes where human striving, wisdom, pleasure, and toil are all declared hevel 'under the sun.' ### G0906 / Sense 63895 (57 occ, sense 1 of 6)

**Description:** To propel something through the air with force; physical throwing or casting of objects (stones, nets, people) into locations (sea, fire, prison), including figurative casting out (fear, figs from a tree).
